The problem

macOS greys out the volume for most displays.

HDMI and DisplayPort outputs usually have no hardware volume control, so the slider is disabled and the volume keys show a crossed-out speaker. You end up reaching for the monitor's buttons.

BeforeVolume slider disabledKeys do nothing on an HDMI display. Volume lives in the monitor's on-screen menu.
With MelicumNative slider and volume keysSoftware gain on the digital stream, kept in sync with the macOS volume and mute controls.

How it works

One tap, one callback, one clock.

Melicum captures system audio with Core Audio process taps, processes it in a realtime C engine and plays it on the display, sharing the display's clock to avoid drift.

  1. Your appsMusic, video and calls play to the Melicum output.
  2. Process tapCore Audio captures them, excluding Melicum itself to prevent feedback. The macOS slider and volume keys control this output.
  3. Melicum engineRealtime C with no allocations: volume, EQ, mixer, reverb, Audio Units and peak protection.
  4. DisplayThe HDMI or DisplayPort output plays the result and provides the clock.

Gain applied once

The optional virtual output exposes standard volume and mute to macOS. Its loopback is unused; Melicum applies the gain in its own engine, never twice.

Safe to stop

Disabling or quitting closes the taps and restores the previous output. If Melicum exits unexpectedly, the virtual output disappears within five seconds.

Local only

Audio is processed in memory on your Mac. Nothing is recorded to disk or sent over the network.

Settings

Your setup, ready when you need it.

Audio presets and application preferences are independent, so recalling an EQ setup does not change your language or startup choices.

Save the whole sound

Super presets include volume, EQ, app levels, reverb and Audio Unit parameters. Link one to an output or an application, or import and export settings as JSON.

Choose how it starts

Launch at login opens Melicum when you sign in. Enable audio on launch separately activates the saved output; it is off by default. If that output is unavailable, processing stays off.

Five languages

Switch immediately between English, Italian, French, German and Spanish under Presets. Read the full guide for every control and preset setting.

Signed updates

Automatic checks. Installation with your approval.

Melicum checks for stable releases approximately once a day by default. Turn automatic checks on or off under Presets → Updates, or check manually from the app or quick menu.

A verified download

Sparkle verifies the signed feed, archive signature and Apple code signing. Drafts and prereleases are excluded from the stable update feed.

Your settings stay

The app asks before downloading and installing. It restores the physical output before quitting, retains presets and preferences, and follows your startup choice after relaunch.

The audio component

App updates do not silently replace the optional HAL component. When a release includes a component update, run its included Enable system volume… installer separately.

Install

Four steps to a working volume key.

  1. Download and open

    Get the latest .dmg from Releases and drag Melicum to Applications. Builds are signed and notarized by Apple.

  2. Pick your display

    Choose the monitor or audio device under Audio output.

  3. Enable audio

    Click Enable audio and allow system audio recording when macOS asks.

  4. Unlock the volume keys

    Click Enable system volume… and authorize the installer. The macOS slider and keys now control your display.

Build from source

Swift, Core Audio and C.

Requires Xcode. The system volume component builds on BlackHole, included as a submodule.

# clone with the BlackHole submodule
git clone --recurse-submodules \
  https://github.com/debba/melicum.git
cd melicum

# test, then build the driver and the app
swift test
./scripts/build-system-volume.sh
./scripts/build.sh
open build/Melicum.app
The name

melicusLatin · "lyrical, for singing"

Melic poetry was sung to the lyre. The icon draws one as an equalizer: the yoke is an EQ curve with three hollow nodes, each string ends on that curve like a frequency band, and the sound box has the profile of an HDMI receptacle.
